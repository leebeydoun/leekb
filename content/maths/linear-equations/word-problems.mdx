Systems of equations earn their keep on questions phrased in words: prices, mixtures, speeds, ages.
The algebra is the easy part, since the earlier pages already cover it. The real skill is turning
the story into equations, and then checking that the answer makes sense in the story. This page
works through the common kinds, one at a time.

## A method

:::remark[Solving a word problem]{#word-problem-steps}

1. **Name the unknowns.** Say exactly what each letter stands for, with units: "let $$a$$ be the
   number of adult tickets", not just "let $$a$$ be adults".
2. **Write one equation per fact.** Each sentence that gives a number usually gives one equation.
   Two unknowns need two independent facts.
3. **Solve** the system, by elimination or substitution.
4. **Answer the question asked,** in words and with units.
5. **Check** the answer against the story, not just the equations: it should satisfy every fact, and
   make sense (no negative ticket counts, no fractions of a person).

:::

Two kinds of fact come up again and again. A **counting** fact says how many things there are in
total, like $$a + c = 250$$. A **value** fact says what they are worth in total, and multiplies each
count by its value per item, like $$8a + 5c = 1640$$.

## Tickets: counting and value

:::example[Theatre tickets]

A school play sold $$250$$ tickets. Adult tickets cost £8 and child tickets £5, and the takings were
£1640. How many of each were sold?

**Unknowns.** Let $$a$$ be the number of adult tickets and $$c$$ the number of child tickets.

**Equations.** There are $$250$$ tickets in all, and the adult tickets bring in $$8a$$ pounds and
the child tickets $$5c$$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
a + c &= 250 &\quad (1) \\
8a + 5c &= 1640 &\quad (2)
\end{aligned}
```

**Solve.** From (1), $$c = 250 - a$$. Substituting into (2), $$8a + 1250 - 5a = 1640$$, so
$$3a = 390$$ and $$a = 130$$. Then $$c = 120$$.

**Answer.** $$130$$ adult tickets and $$120$$ child tickets.

**Check.** $$130 + 120 = 250$$ tickets, and $$130 \cdot 8 + 120 \cdot 5 = 1040 + 600 = 1640$$
pounds.

:::

## Mixtures

Mixture problems are the same shape: an amount of each ingredient, and a total value.

:::example[Blending coffee]

A roaster blends beans costing £12 per kilogram with beans costing £20 per kilogram, to make $$40$$
kg of a blend worth £15 per kilogram. How much of each goes in?

**Unknowns.** Let $$x$$ be the kilograms of £12 beans and $$y$$ the kilograms of £20 beans.

**Equations.** The weights add up to $$40$$ kg, and the values add up to the value of the blend,
$$40 \cdot 15 = 600$$ pounds:

```math
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 40 &\quad (1) \\
12x + 20y &= 600 &\quad (2)
\end{aligned}
```

**Solve.** Multiplying (1) by $$12$$ and subtracting from (2): $$8y = 120$$, so $$y = 15$$ and
$$x = 25$$.

**Answer.** $$25$$ kg of the £12 beans and $$15$$ kg of the £20 beans.

**Check.** $$25 + 15 = 40$$ kg, worth $$25 \cdot 12 + 15 \cdot 20 = 300 + 300 = 600$$ pounds, which
is £15 per kilogram.

:::

A quick sense check: the blend price, £15, is nearer to £12 than to £20, so there should be more of
the cheaper beans. There are.

## Speeds with and against a current

Distance, speed and time are linked by

```math
\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}
```

A current, or a wind, adds to a speed in one direction and takes away from it in the other.

:::example[A boat on a river]

A boat travels $$36$$ km downstream in $$2$$ hours, and makes the same trip back upstream in $$3$$
hours. How fast does the boat go in still water, and how fast is the current?

**Unknowns.** Let $$b$$ be the boat's speed in still water and $$s$$ the speed of the current, both
in km per hour.

**Equations.** Going downstream the current helps, so the speed is $$b + s$$. Going upstream it
hinders, so the speed is $$b - s$$. Each trip is $$36$$ km:

```math
\begin{aligned}
2(b + s) &= 36 \\
3(b - s) &= 36
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
\begin{aligned}
b + s &= 18 \\
b - s &= 12
\end{aligned}
```

**Solve.** Adding, $$2b = 30$$, so $$b = 15$$, and then $$s = 3$$.

**Answer.** The boat goes $$15$$ km per hour in still water, and the current flows at $$3$$ km per
hour.

**Check.** Downstream, $$18$$ km per hour for $$2$$ hours is $$36$$ km. Upstream, $$12$$ km per hour
for $$3$$ hours is $$36$$ km.

:::

## Ages

Age problems compare people at two different times. The trick is that in $$n$$ years, **everyone**
is $$n$$ years older.

:::example[Ages]

Ana is three times as old as her brother. In six years' time, she will be twice as old as him. How
old are they now?

**Unknowns.** Let $$a$$ be Ana's age now and $$b$$ her brother's, in years.

**Equations.** Now, $$a = 3b$$. In six years they will be $$a + 6$$ and $$b + 6$$, so
$$a + 6 = 2(b + 6)$$.

**Solve.** Substituting $$a = 3b$$: $$3b + 6 = 2b + 12$$, so $$b = 6$$ and $$a = 18$$.

**Answer.** Ana is $$18$$ and her brother is $$6$$.

**Check.** $$18 = 3 \cdot 6$$, and in six years $$24 = 2 \cdot 12$$.

:::

## When the answer doesn't make sense

The algebra always produces numbers, but the story may rule them out. Then the honest answer is that
the situation described can't happen.

:::example[An impossible bag of coins]

A bag holds $$20$$ coins, all 10p and 50p pieces, worth £12 altogether. How many of each are there?

Let $$x$$ be the number of 10p coins and $$y$$ the number of 50p coins. Working in pence,

```math
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 20 \\
10x + 50y &= 1200
\end{aligned}
```

Substituting $$y = 20 - x$$: $$10x + 1000 - 50x = 1200$$, so $$-40x = 200$$ and $$x = -5$$. Then
$$y = 25$$.

The system has a solution, but a bag can't hold $$-5$$ coins. Even $$20$$ coins that were all 50p
pieces would only make £10, so no such bag exists. Checking against the story caught what the
algebra couldn't.

:::

## Common mistakes

- **Vague unknowns.** "Let $$x$$ be coffee" leads to mixing up kilograms and pounds. Say what is
  being counted, and in what units.
- **Mixing units.** Pounds and pence, or minutes and hours, in the same equation give nonsense.
  Convert everything first.
- **Adding prices instead of values.** In a mixture, the value equation multiplies each amount by
  its price; the prices themselves don't add up to anything meaningful.
- **Forgetting to age everyone.** In "in six years' time", both people get six years older.
- **Skipping the story check.** A negative or fractional count means a mistake, or an impossible
  situation.

## In short

- Name each unknown precisely, with units, and write one equation per fact.
- Most problems combine a **counting** equation (how many in total) with a **value** equation (what
  they are worth, or how far they go, in total).
- Answer the question that was asked, and check the answer against the story, not just the
  equations.
