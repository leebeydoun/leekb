Draw two dots and two crossing lines on a sheet of paper. You can measure how far apart the dots
are, and how wide the corner is where the lines cross. Those two measurements, distance and angle,
are what geometry is built on.

The earlier chapters worked with numbers, and this one moves onto the **plane**. The plane is the
sheet of paper made perfect: completely flat, with no edges, and running on without end in every
direction. A page or a screen shows a piece of it.

In this chapter we'll take a few facts that match the picture in your head and prove the Pythagoras
theorem from them. The same facts then explain perpendicular bisectors, the shortest distance from a
point to a line, and the angles and area of any triangle.

Geometry works the same way as the rest of these notes. A small number of facts are taken as given,
such as "two points lie on exactly one line", and each guide marks them clearly in a box. Everything
else is proved from them, using the methods from the
[Basic proofs](../basic-proofs/introduction.mdx) chapter. Lengths and areas are real numbers, so
square roots from the [Real numbers](../real-numbers/introduction.mdx) chapter appear throughout.

Later chapters prove some of these facts. The
[Area and scaling](../area-and-scaling/introduction.mdx) chapter proves the rules about the area of
a disc. The [Coordinates](../coordinates/introduction.mdx) chapter proves the rules for distance,
and the [Segments, rays and lines](../segments-rays-and-lines/introduction.mdx) chapter proves the
rules for lines, segments and perpendicular lines. The [Isometries](../isometries/introduction.mdx)
chapter, which comes next, shows that the rule for right triangles with the same legs is one case of
congruence.

**Definitions**

| Term                          | Meaning                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#segment}                | The straight piece of the line $$PM$$ from $$P$$ to $$M$$, of length $$d(P, M)$$                                    |
| :ref{#circle}                 | Circle: points with $$d(P, Q) = r$$; disc: points with $$d(P, Q) \le r$$                                            |
| :ref{#ray}                    | The ray $$PQ$$ starts at $$P$$ and runs on through $$Q$$                                                            |
| :ref{#perpendicular}          | Lines that cross making four equal angles, written $$L_1 \perp L_2$$                                                |
| :ref{#rectangle}              | Four sides, each perpendicular to the two next to it; a square has four equal sides                                 |
| :ref{#angle}                  | One of the two regions that two rays from a point $$P$$ split the plane into                                        |
| :ref{#special-angles}         | Zero and full: the rays are the same; straight: the rays point in opposite directions                               |
| :ref{#angle-name}             | $$\angle QPM$$ is the angle up to a straight angle; the other is the reflex angle                                   |
| :ref{#sector}                 | The part of a disc centred at the vertex that lies inside the angle                                                 |
| :ref{#degree}                 | An angle of $$x^\circ$$ is $$\frac{x}{360}$$ of a full turn, so its sector is $$\frac{x}{360}$$ of the disc         |
| :ref{#right-angle}            | $$90^\circ$$; acute is under $$90^\circ$$, obtuse between $$90^\circ$$ and $$180^\circ$$, reflex over $$180^\circ$$ |
| :ref{#triangle}               | The three segments joining three points not on one line                                                             |
| :ref{#right-triangle}         | A triangle with a right angle; legs $$a$$, $$b$$ meet there, the hypotenuse $$c$$ faces it                          |
| :ref{#midpoint}               | The point $$O$$ of the segment $$PQ$$ with $$d(P, O) = d(O, Q)$$                                                    |
| :ref{#perpendicular-bisector} | The line through the midpoint of $$PQ$$ perpendicular to $$PQ$$                                                     |
| :ref{#point-line-distance}    | $$d(P, F)$$, where $$F$$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $$P$$                                                |
| :ref{#height}                 | The height is the distance from the opposite corner to the line of the base                                         |

**Distance**

| Rule                  | Statement                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#distance-rules} | $$d(P, Q) \ge 0$$, with $$0$$ exactly when $$P = Q$$; $$d(P, Q) = d(Q, P)$$; $$d(P, M) \le d(P, Q) + d(Q, M)$$                                                               |
| :ref{#segment-rules}  | $$d(P, M) = d(P, Q) + d(Q, M)$$ exactly when $$Q$$ is on $$PM$$; each length from $$0$$ to $$d(P, M)$$ is reached once; of three points on a line, one is between the others |

**Lines and rays**

| Rule                           | Statement                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-rules}              | One line through two points; one parallel through a point                                            |
| :ref{#crossing-lines}          | Different lines meet at most once; lines that are not parallel meet once                             |
| :ref{#parallel-transitive}     | If $$L_1 \parallel L_2$$ and $$L_2 \parallel L_3$$, then $$L_1 \parallel L_3$$                       |
| :ref{#perpendicular-rules}     | One perpendicular through a point; perpendicular to one of two parallels means perpendicular to both |
| :ref{#perpendiculars-parallel} | Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel                                                |
| :ref{#parallel-distance}       | Perpendicular segments between two parallel lines all have the same length                           |
| :ref{#rectangle-sides}         | Opposite sides of a rectangle have the same length                                                   |
| :ref{#area-rules}              | A rectangle has area $$ab$$; the areas of pieces that don't overlap add                              |

**Measuring angles**

| Rule                  | Statement                                                                                                              |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#disc-area}      | Area $$\pi r^2$$; the fraction a sector takes up doesn't depend on the radius; a line through the centre halves a disc |
| :ref{#angle-addition} | If the ray $$PR$$ is inside $$\angle QPM$$, then $$\angle QPR + \angle RPM = \angle QPM$$                              |
| :ref{#straight-angle} | Every straight angle is $$180^\circ$$                                                                                  |
| :ref{#angles-on-line} | Two angles side by side on a straight line add up to $$180^\circ$$                                                     |
| :ref{#sector-area}    | A sector of $$x^\circ$$ in a disc of radius $$r$$ has area $$\frac{x}{360} \pi r^2$$                                   |

**Right triangles**

| Rule                         | Statement                                                                    |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#right-triangle-rule}   | Right triangles with the same legs have the same hypotenuse, angles and area |
| :ref{#right-triangle-angles} | The two angles other than the right angle add up to $$90^\circ$$             |
| :ref{#right-triangle-area}   | A right triangle with legs $$a$$ and $$b$$ has area $$\frac{ab}{2}$$         |

**The Pythagoras theorem**

| Rule                    | Statement                                             |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#pythagoras}       | $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$                                   |
| :ref{#pythagoras-sides} | $$c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$$ and $$a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$$ |

**Perpendicular bisectors**

| Rule                               | Statement                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#equidistant-on-line}         | A point of the line $$PQ$$ equally far from $$P$$ and $$Q$$ is the midpoint                                           |
| :ref{#perpendicular-bisector-rule} | $$d(P, M) = d(Q, M)$$ exactly when $$M$$ is on the perpendicular bisector of $$PQ$$                                   |
| :ref{#hypotenuse-longest}          | The hypotenuse is longer than each leg                                                                                |
| :ref{#shortest-distance}           | The closest point of a line to $$P$$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $$P$$                                      |
| :ref{#circumcentre}                | The perpendicular bisectors of a triangle's sides meet at one point, the centre of a circle through all three corners |

**Angles in triangles**

| Rule                      | Statement                                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#opposite-angles}    | Where two lines cross, opposite angles are equal                                               |
| :ref{#triangle-angle-sum} | The angles of a triangle add up to $$180^\circ$$                                               |
| :ref{#parallel-angles}    | A line crossing two parallel lines makes equal alternate angles and equal corresponding angles |
| :ref{#triangle-area}      | A triangle with base $$b$$ and height $$h$$ has area $$\frac{1}{2}bh$$                         |
