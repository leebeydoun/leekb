title: Introduction
description: Naming points by numbers, so that distance, circles and right triangles become algebra.

Imagine your cinema ticket says row 7, seat 12, and your friend's says row 10, seat 8. Before either
of you has found your seat, you already know how far apart you'll be. You are $$3$$ rows apart and
$$4$$ seats apart, so if the rows are spaced like the seats, the straight line between you is $$5$$
seats long, by Pythagoras. Numbers that name a place like this are called **coordinates**. In this
chapter we'll give every point of the plane a pair of numbers, and turn questions about distance,
circles and right triangles into algebra.

The picture to keep in mind is a sheet of graph paper with two axes drawn on it. Every point has a
number across and a number up, and the distance between two points comes from their numbers, with
the Pythagoras theorem. Later we turn the picture round: start from the pairs of numbers, define
distance by the formula, and prove facts about distance with algebra, the way a computer would check
them.

As in the earlier chapters, a few facts are taken as given and marked clearly in a box, such as
"each point has exactly one pair of coordinates". Everything else is proved from them, using the
distances, circles and the Pythagoras theorem of
[Distance and angles](../distance-and-angles/introduction.mdx), the slides and congruent triangles
of [Isometries](../isometries/introduction.mdx), the zooms of
[Area and scaling](../area-and-scaling/introduction.mdx) and the completed squares of
[Quadratics](../quadratics/introduction.mdx). This chapter also settles two questions those chapters
left open:

- **The rules for distance**: Distance and angles took three rules as given: a distance is never
  negative, it is the same both ways, and a detour is never shorter. Here all three are proved from
  the distance formula, the detour rule in full, in the plane and in space.
- **Zooms and distance**: Area and scaling took it as given that a zoom by $$r$$ multiplies every
  distance by $$r$$, until coordinates arrived. Here that rule is proved, for a zoom about any
  centre.

Lines in coordinates wait for a later chapter. That chapter finds the equation of the line through
two points, works out where two lines meet and when they are perpendicular, measures angles from
coordinates, and adds points together like numbers. Until then, the guides use an equation such as $$y = 2x + 1$$ only
as in [Linear equations](../linear-equations/introduction.mdx), where its graph is a straight line
taken from the picture. So the other two rules for zooms, that lines go to lines and angles are
kept, are still taken as given.

## Guides in this chapter

The chapter has eight guides:

1. [Coordinates](./coordinates.mdx): an origin and two axes, the pair of numbers for each point,
   quadrants, and what a different choice of axes does to the numbers
2. [The distance between two points](./distance.mdx): the distance formula from Pythagoras, care
   with minus signs, and comparing squared distances to save a square root
3. [Geometry from numbers](./geometry-from-numbers.mdx): the plane as pairs of numbers, the three
   rules for distance proved with algebra, and zooms and slides in coordinates
4. [The equation of a circle](./circles.mdx): circles and discs as equations and inequalities,
   multiplied-out equations, and where a circle meets a horizontal line
5. [Whole-number right triangles](./pythagorean-triples.mdx): why three lengths can prove a right
   angle, and every Pythagorean triple from lines through one point of a circle
6. [Points in space](./space.mdx): three coordinates, distance in space, spheres, and the rules for
   distance with a third term
7. [Test yourself](./test-yourself.mdx): a multiple choice quiz on the ideas in the chapter
8. [Exercises](./exercises.mdx): exercises on the whole chapter, with worked solutions

:::accordion[Summary of definitions and rules]

**Definitions**

| Term                      | Meaning                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#coordinates}        | $$P = (x, y)$$: the numbers at the feet of the perpendiculars from $$P$$ to the $$x$$-axis and $$y$$-axis  |
| :ref{#quadrants}          | The four regions off the axes, numbered anticlockwise from the first, where $$x > 0$$ and $$y > 0$$        |
| :ref{#coordinate-plane}   | $$\mathbb{R}^2$$: all pairs $$(x, y)$$ of real numbers, with distance defined by the distance formula      |
| :ref{#pythagorean-triple} | Positive integers $$a$$, $$b$$, $$c$$ with $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$                                             |
| :ref{#rational-point}     | A point whose two coordinates are both rational numbers                                                    |
| :ref{#sphere}             | Sphere: points with $$d(C, P) = r$$; ball: points with $$d(C, P) \le r$$                                   |
| :ref{#coordinate-space}   | $$\mathbb{R}^3$$: all triples $$(x, y, z)$$ of real numbers, with distance defined by the formula in space |

**Coordinates**

| Rule                    | Statement                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#coordinate-rules} | One pair for each point and one point for each pair; $$(a, 0)$$ and $$(c, 0)$$ are $$\lvert a - c \rvert$$ apart; $$x = a$$ and $$y = b$$ are perpendicular lines |

**The distance between two points**

| Rule                    | Statement                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-distance}    | $$\sqrt{(a - b)^2} = \lvert a - b \rvert$$; level points $$(x_1, c)$$, $$(x_2, c)$$ are $$\lvert x_1 - x_2 \rvert$$ apart |
| :ref{#distance-formula} | $$d(P, Q) = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$                                                                        |

**Geometry from numbers**

| Rule                             | Statement                                                             |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#distance-zero}             | $$d(P, Q) \ge 0$$, with $$0$$ exactly when $$P = Q$$                  |
| :ref{#distance-symmetric}        | $$d(P, Q) = d(Q, P)$$                                                 |
| :ref{#product-bound}             | $$as + bt \le \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\,\sqrt{s^2 + t^2}$$                    |
| :ref{#triangle-inequality-plane} | $$d(P, M) \le d(P, Q) + d(Q, M)$$                                     |
| :ref{#origin-zoom}               | With $$rP = (rx, ry)$$ and $$r > 0$$, $$d(rP, rQ) = r \cdot d(P, Q)$$ |
| :ref{#slide-distance}            | The slide $$(x, y) \mapsto (x + h, y + k)$$ keeps every distance      |

**The equation of a circle**

| Rule                          | Statement                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| :ref{#circle-equation}        | $$(x, y)$$ is on the circle with centre $$(a, b)$$ and radius $$r$$ exactly when $$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$$               |
| :ref{#disc-inequality}        | In the disc exactly when $$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 \le r^2$$; inside with $$<$$, outside with $$>$$                              |
| :ref{#expanded-circle}        | $$x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$$ is a circle, one point or nothing as $$\frac{D^2 + E^2}{4} - F$$ is positive, zero or negative |
| :ref{#circle-horizontal-line} | The line $$y = k$$ meets the circle twice, once or never as $$\lvert k - b \rvert$$ is less than, equal to or more than $$r$$  |

**Whole-number right triangles**

| Rule                       | Statement                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#pythagoras-converse} | If $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$, the angle opposite the side $$c$$ is a right angle                                                                                             |
| :ref{#triples-points}      | A triple $$a$$, $$b$$, $$c$$ gives the rational point $$(\frac{a}{c}, \frac{b}{c})$$ on the unit circle, and each such point with positive coordinates gives a triple  |
| :ref{#rational-points}     | The line $$y = t(x + 1)$$ meets the unit circle again at $$\left(\frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}, \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}\right)$$, a rational point exactly when $$t$$ is rational |
| :ref{#triple-formula}      | $$n^2 - m^2$$, $$2mn$$, $$n^2 + m^2$$ is a triple for $$m < n$$, and every triple is a multiple of one of these                                                        |

**Points in space**

| Rule                          | Statement                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#space-coordinate-rules} | Three perpendicular axes; one triple for each point; each layer $$z = c$$ is a plane with coordinates $$x$$ and $$y$$ |
| :ref{#space-distance}         | $$d(P, Q) = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$$                                                    |
| :ref{#sphere-equation}        | On the sphere exactly when $$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 + (z - c)^2 = r^2$$, in the ball with $$\le$$                      |
| :ref{#product-bound-space}    | $$ap + bq + cs \le \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}\,\sqrt{p^2 + q^2 + s^2}$$                                                   |
| :ref{#space-distance-rules}   | In $$\mathbb{R}^3$$, distance is never negative, the same both ways, and obeys the triangle inequality                |
| :ref{#space-zoom}             | In $$\mathbb{R}^3$$, $$d(rP, rQ) = r \cdot d(P, Q)$$ for $$r > 0$$                                                    |

:::
