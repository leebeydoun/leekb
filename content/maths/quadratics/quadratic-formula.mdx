[Completing the square](./completing-the-square.mdx) solves any quadratic, but it is the same few
steps every time: divide by the number in front of $$x^2$$, add the missing corner, take square
roots. Steps that never change can be done once and for all, with letters in place of the numbers.
The result is the quadratic formula, which goes straight from the three numbers in an equation to
its solutions. This page derives it, shows how to use it safely, and says when another method is
quicker.

## Doing the steps once with letters

Picture following a recipe so often that you write down the finished dish in terms of its
ingredients. Every quadratic $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$ has the same three ingredients, the numbers
$$a$$, $$b$$ and $$c$$. Completing the square with these letters, instead of particular numbers,
does every quadratic at once, and the answer comes out as a formula in $$a$$, $$b$$ and $$c$$.

The formula also has a shape worth seeing. The solutions sit symmetrically about one point,
$$-\frac{b}{2a}$$: one is a certain distance to its right and the other the same distance to its
left. The formula gives that centre, plus or minus that distance. When the distance would be the
square root of a negative number, there are no solutions at all.

:::theorem[The quadratic formula]{#quadratic-formula}

Let $$a$$, $$b$$ and $$c$$ be real numbers with $$a \neq 0$$, and consider the quadratic equation
$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$ (:ref{#quadratic}).

1. If $$b^2 - 4ac \ge 0$$, its solutions are exactly the numbers given by

   ```math
   x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
   ```

   that is, $$\frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$ and $$\frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$.

2. If $$b^2 - 4ac < 0$$, it has no real solution.

:::

:::proof{source="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_formula#Derivations"}

Since $$a \neq 0$$, dividing both sides by $$a$$ is a safe step
(:ref{#linear-equations/safe-steps}), and subtracting $$\frac{c}{a}$$ from both sides is another. So
the equation is equivalent to

```math
x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x = -\frac{c}{a}
```

Now complete the square (:ref{#complete-square}). Half of $$\frac{b}{a}$$ is $$\frac{b}{2a}$$, so
adding its square $$\frac{b^2}{4a^2}$$ to both sides turns the left side into a square:

```math
\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2}{4a^2} - \frac{c}{a} = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}
```

where the right side was put over the common denominator $$4a^2$$.

The denominator $$4a^2$$ is positive, since $$a \neq 0$$ (:ref{#real-numbers/squares-positive}). So
the right side has the same sign as $$b^2 - 4ac$$.

**Part 2.** If $$b^2 - 4ac < 0$$, the right side is negative. The left side is a square, which is
never negative (:ref{#real-numbers/squares-positive}), so no $$x$$ makes the two sides equal.

**Part 1.** If $$b^2 - 4ac \ge 0$$, the right side is not negative, and solving a shifted square
(:ref{#shifted-square}) gives

```math
x + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{\sqrt{4a^2}}
```

by :ref{#real-numbers/root-product}. Here $$\sqrt{4a^2}$$ is $$2a$$ if $$a > 0$$ and $$-2a$$ if
$$a < 0$$. Either way the $$\pm$$ already covers both signs, so the two values are
$$\pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$. Subtracting $$\frac{b}{2a}$$ from both sides,

```math
x = -\frac{b}{2a} \pm \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
```

Every step was safe, so these are exactly the solutions of the original equation.

:::

The number $$b^2 - 4ac$$ under the root decides everything about how many solutions there are, and
the [next page](./discriminant.mdx) gives it a name and a closer look.

## Using the formula

The formula needs the equation in the standard form $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$, with everything on one
side and $$0$$ on the other. Then read off $$a$$, $$b$$ and $$c$$, **signs included**, and work out
$$b^2 - 4ac$$ first. Its sign says at once whether there are any solutions, and its square root is
the only awkward part of the calculation.

:::example[Answers without a square root]

Solve $$3x^2 + 5x - 2 = 0$$.

Here $$a = 3$$, $$b = 5$$ and $$c = -2$$. First,

```math
b^2 - 4ac = 5^2 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot (-2) = 25 + 24 = 49
```

which is positive, with $$\sqrt{49} = 7$$. So

```math
x = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{2 \cdot 3} = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{6}
```

giving $$x = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$ and $$x = \frac{-12}{6} = -2$$.

**Check:** $$3 \cdot \frac{1}{9} + 5 \cdot \frac{1}{3} - 2 = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{5}{3} - 2 = 0$$,
and $$3 \cdot 4 - 10 - 2 = 0$$.

:::

:::example[Answers with a square root]

Solve $$x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0$$.

Here $$a = 1$$, $$b = -6$$ and $$c = 4$$, so $$b^2 - 4ac = 36 - 16 = 20$$. Simplifying the root,
$$\sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4}\sqrt{5} = 2\sqrt{5}$$ (:ref{#real-numbers/root-product}), and

```math
x = \frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{5}
```

Both terms on top were divided by $$2$$. The solutions are $$3 + \sqrt{5}$$ and $$3 - \sqrt{5}$$,
symmetric about $$-\frac{b}{2a} = 3$$.

**Check:** $$(3 + \sqrt{5})^2 = 9 + 6\sqrt{5} + 5 = 14 + 6\sqrt{5}$$, so
$$(3 + \sqrt{5})^2 - 6(3 + \sqrt{5}) + 4 = 14 + 6\sqrt{5} - 18 - 6\sqrt{5} + 4 = 0$$. The other
solution works the same way with the signs of $$\sqrt{5}$$ swapped.

:::

:::example[First rearrange]

Solve $$3x^2 = 4x + 1$$.

The formula needs $$0$$ on one side, so subtract $$4x + 1$$ from both sides: $$3x^2 - 4x - 1 = 0$$.
Now $$a = 3$$, $$b = -4$$, $$c = -1$$, and

```math
b^2 - 4ac = 16 + 12 = 28, \qquad \sqrt{28} = 2\sqrt{7}
```

So

```math
x = \frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{7}}{6} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{7}}{3}
```

:::

:::example[No real solution]

Solve $$5x^2 + 2x + 1 = 0$$.

Here $$b^2 - 4ac = 4 - 20 = -16$$, which is negative. By part 2 of :ref{#quadratic-formula}, there
is no real solution, and there is nothing more to calculate.

:::

## Checking an answer

An answer with a square root in it is easy to get slightly wrong, and slow to check by substituting.
There is a quicker check. For the two solutions of $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$, their sum is always
$$-\frac{b}{a}$$ and their product is always $$\frac{c}{a}$$ (:ref{#vieta}, on the next page).

For $$3x^2 = 4x + 1$$ above, the solutions were $$\frac{2 + \sqrt{7}}{3}$$ and
$$\frac{2 - \sqrt{7}}{3}$$. Their sum is $$\frac{4}{3}$$, which is $$-\frac{b}{a} = \frac{4}{3}$$.
Their product is $$\frac{4 - 7}{9} = -\frac{1}{3}$$, using the difference of two squares
(:ref{#numbers/square-formulas}), and that is $$\frac{c}{a} = -\frac{1}{3}$$. Both agree, so the
answer is almost certainly right.

## When to use which method

The formula always works, which makes it the safe choice. But it is not always the quickest.

- **Factoring** (:ref{#solve-by-factors}) is quicker when the solutions are whole numbers or simple
  fractions you can spot. For $$x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$$, the numbers $$2$$ and $$3$$ have sum $$5$$ and
  product $$6$$, so it is $$(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$$, with no square roots at all.
- **With no $$x$$ term**, as in $$x^2 - 7 = 0$$, solve $$x^2 = 7$$ directly: $$x = \pm\sqrt{7}$$.
- **With no constant term**, as in $$4x^2 + 3x = 0$$, take out the common factor: $$x(4x + 3) = 0$$,
  so $$x = 0$$ or $$x = -\frac{3}{4}$$.
- **Otherwise**, or when factors don't show themselves after a short look, use the formula.

The formula also covers equations whose numbers aren't whole. For $$x^2 + \sqrt{2}\,x - 4 = 0$$,
$$b^2 - 4ac = 2 + 16 = 18$$ and $$\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$$, so
$$x = \frac{-\sqrt{2} \pm 3\sqrt{2}}{2}$$, which is $$\sqrt{2}$$ or $$-2\sqrt{2}$$.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the formula before rearranging.** In $$3x^2 = 4x + 1$$, the numbers $$3$$, $$4$$ and $$1$$
  are not $$a$$, $$b$$ and $$c$$. Move everything to one side first.
- **Losing a minus sign.** With $$b = -6$$, the top starts with $$-b = 6$$, and $$b^2 = 36$$, not
  $$-36$$. Put negative numbers in brackets when substituting.
- **Dividing only part of the top by $$2a$$.** The whole of $$-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$$ is divided
  by $$2a$$, not just the square root.
- **Writing $$a$$ instead of $$2a$$ underneath.** The bottom is $$2a$$, from the half taken when
  completing the square.
- **Splitting the root.** $$\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$$ is not $$b - 2\sqrt{ac}$$; roots don't split over
  sums or differences.

## In short

- Completing the square on $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$ once, with letters, gives the quadratic formula
  $$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$.
- If $$b^2 - 4ac < 0$$ there is no real solution; otherwise the formula gives every solution.
- The solutions sit symmetrically about $$-\frac{b}{2a}$$.
- Put the equation in the form $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$ first, read off the signs carefully, and work
  out $$b^2 - 4ac$$ before anything else.
- Check answers with the sum $$-\frac{b}{a}$$ and product $$\frac{c}{a}$$ of the solutions.
- Factoring or taking square roots directly can be quicker; the formula always works.
