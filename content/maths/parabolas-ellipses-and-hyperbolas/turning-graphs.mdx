  The rules for sliding, stretching and flipping a graph gathered together, turning a graph about
  the origin, and the recipe for moving any curve.

Imagine the cup $$y = x^2$$ drawn on a sheet of tracing paper, pinned to the page at the origin.
Turn the sheet a quarter turn anticlockwise and the cup lies on its side, opening to the left. The
curve keeps its shape, but its equation changes. In this guide we'll find the new equation for any
turn, and gather it with the other moves into one recipe that the rest of the chapter uses.

## The moves so far

The [Moving graphs](../functions/moving-graphs.mdx) guide found what three kinds of move do to an
equation $$E(x, y)$$. The slide by $$(h, k)$$ puts $$x - h$$ for $$x$$ and $$y - k$$ for $$y$$
(:ref{#functions/slide-graph}). The stretch $$D_{a,b}(x, y) = (ax, by)$$ puts $$\frac{x}{a}$$ for
$$x$$ and $$\frac{y}{b}$$ for $$y$$ (:ref{#functions/stretch-graph}). The flips change the sign of a
letter, or swap the two letters for the flip in $$y = x$$ (:ref{#functions/flip-graph}).

All three follow one pattern: to move a graph, put in the point moved back. For example, the slide
by $$(3, -1)$$ sends the circle $$x^2 + y^2 = 4$$ onto $$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 4$$. Turns are the
one move still missing, because a turn by an angle needs sine and cosine to write down.

## Turning a graph

A turn works the same way, with one more step to find the point moved back. As in the Trigonometry
chapter, $$G_\varphi$$ is the turn about $$O$$ by $$\varphi$$ radians, anticlockwise when
$$\varphi > 0$$ (:ref{#trigonometry/radians-turn-notation}). To take a point back after
$$G_\varphi$$, you turn it by $$-\varphi$$, the same amount the other way. By
:ref{#trigonometry/rotation-formula} with $$-\varphi$$ in place of $$\varphi$$, and
$$\cos(-\varphi) = \cos\varphi$$ and $$\sin(-\varphi) = -\sin\varphi$$ from
:ref{#trigonometry/negative-angle},

```math
G_{-\varphi}(x, y) = (x\cos\varphi + y\sin\varphi,\ -x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi)
```

:::theorem[Turning a graph]{#turn-graph}

Let $$\varphi$$ be a number. The turn $$G_\varphi$$ about $$O$$ sends the graph of an equation
$$E(x, y)$$ onto the graph of

```math
E(x\cos\varphi + y\sin\varphi,\ -x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi)
```

That is, put in the point turned back by $$\varphi$$.

:::

:::proof{source="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_of_axes"}

Let $$C$$ be the graph of $$E(x, y)$$ and $$C'$$ the graph of the new equation. By the formula for
$$G_{-\varphi}$$ above, a point $$Q$$ is on $$C'$$ exactly when $$G_{-\varphi}(Q)$$ is on $$C$$.
Turns add, and the turn by $$0$$ is the identity $$I$$ (parts 1 and 2 of
:ref{#trigonometry/radian-turns}), so

```math
G_{-\varphi} \circ G_\varphi = G_0 = I \qquad G_\varphi \circ G_{-\varphi} = G_0 = I
```

**Every turned point is on C′.** Take $$P$$ on $$C$$. Then $$G_{-\varphi}(G_\varphi(P)) = P$$, which
is on $$C$$. So $$G_\varphi(P)$$ is on $$C'$$.

**Every point of C′ is a turned point.** Take $$Q$$ on $$C'$$, and let $$P = G_{-\varphi}(Q)$$,
which is on $$C$$. Then $$G_\varphi(P) = G_\varphi(G_{-\varphi}(Q)) = Q$$. So $$Q$$ is in
$$G_\varphi(C)$$.

:::

Turn $$y = x^2$$ a quarter turn anticlockwise, $$\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$$. Here
$$\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0$$ and $$\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$$, so the turned-back point is

```math
(x \cdot 0 + y \cdot 1,\ -x \cdot 1 + y \cdot 0) = (y, -x)
```

In $$y = x^2$$, the first letter is the one squared and the second stands alone. Putting $$y$$ for
the first and $$-x$$ for the second gives $$-x = y^2$$, which is $$x = -y^2$$: a parabola opening to
the left. That matches the picture of a cup tipped over a quarter turn anticlockwise, which ends up
with its opening facing left.

Check with a point. The point $$(1, 1)$$ is on $$y = x^2$$. The quarter turn sends it to $$(-1, 1)$$
(part 4 of :ref{#trigonometry/radian-turns}), and $$-y^2 = -1$$, so $$(-1, 1)$$ fits $$x = -y^2$$.
The same turn sends $$(2, 4)$$ to $$(-4, 2)$$, and $$-2^2 = -4$$.

## The recipe for every move

The table from [Moving graphs](../functions/moving-graphs.mdx#the-recipe-for-every-move) now gains a
row for turns. Each row shows a move, the point moved back, and what the move does to the parabola
$$y = x^2$$.

| Move                            | Formula                                                         | Put in place of $$x$$           | Put in place of $$y$$            | $$y = x^2$$ becomes                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Slide by $$(h, k)$$             | $$(x + h, y + k)$$                                              | $$x - h$$                       | $$y - k$$                        | $$y - k = (x - h)^2$$                         |
| Stretch $$D_{a,b}$$             | $$(ax, by)$$                                                    | $$\frac{x}{a}$$                 | $$\frac{y}{b}$$                  | $$\frac{y}{b} = \frac{x^2}{a^2}$$             |
| Flip in the $$y$$-axis, $$R_2$$ | $$(-x, y)$$                                                     | $$-x$$                          | $$y$$                            | $$y = x^2$$                                   |
| Flip in the $$x$$-axis, $$R_1$$ | $$(x, -y)$$                                                     | $$x$$                           | $$-y$$                           | $$y = -x^2$$                                  |
| Flip in $$y = x$$, $$S$$        | $$(y, x)$$                                                      | $$y$$                           | $$x$$                            | $$x = y^2$$                                   |
| Turn $$G_\varphi$$ about $$O$$  | $$(x\cos\varphi - y\sin\varphi,\ x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi)$$ | $$x\cos\varphi + y\sin\varphi$$ | $$-x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi$$ | for $$\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$$, $$x = -y^2$$ |

Two moves one after the other means two rules one after the other, and turns combine with the other
moves in the same way. Turn $$y = x^2$$ a quarter turn anticlockwise, giving $$x = -y^2$$, and then
slide it by $$(1, 2)$$. The slide puts $$x - 1$$ for $$x$$ and $$y - 2$$ for $$y$$:

```math
x - 1 = -(y - 2)^2
```

This parabola opens to the left from the point $$(1, 2)$$. Check with a point: the turn sends
$$(1, 1)$$ to $$(-1, 1)$$, and the slide sends that to $$(0, 3)$$. Then $$0 - 1 = -1$$ and
$$-(3 - 2)^2 = -1$$, so $$(0, 3)$$ is on the new curve.

Every later guide in this chapter uses these rules. Each curve starts from one standard equation,
and every other curve of the same kind is that one moved by slides, turns and flips.

## Exercises

:::::accordion[Exercises on turning graphs, with solutions]

::::exercise{#ex-turning-graphs-turn-axis}

Use :ref{#turn-graph} to turn the $$x$$-axis, the graph of $$y = 0$$, by $$\frac{\pi}{4}$$ about
$$O$$. Then show that for any $$\varphi$$ with $$\cos\varphi \ne 0$$ the turned axis is the line
$$y = (\tan\varphi)\,x$$.

:::solution

Here $$E(x, y)$$ is $$y = 0$$, which only involves the second letter. By :ref{#turn-graph}, the
turned graph is $$-x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi = 0$$. With $$\varphi = \frac{\pi}{4}$$,
$$\cos\varphi = \sin\varphi = \frac{\sqrt2}{2}$$, so

```math
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(-x + y) &= 0 && \text{take out } \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} \\
y &= x && \text{divide by } \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} \ne 0 \text{ and add } x
\end{aligned}
```

Check: the turn sends $$(1, 0)$$ to $$(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}, \sin\frac{\pi}{4})$$, which has equal
coordinates. For any $$\varphi$$ with $$\cos\varphi \ne 0$$, divide $$y\cos\varphi = x\sin\varphi$$
by $$\cos\varphi$$ to get $$y = \frac{\sin\varphi}{\cos\varphi}x = (\tan\varphi)\,x$$
(:ref{#trigonometry/tangent}). The turned axis climbs $$\tan\varphi$$ for each step right, as
:ref{#trigonometry/tangent-slope} says.

:::

::::

::::exercise{#ex-turning-graphs-turn-circle}

Use :ref{#turn-graph} to show that every turn about $$O$$ sends the unit circle onto itself.

:::solution

Write $$c = \cos\varphi$$ and $$s = \sin\varphi$$. By :ref{#turn-graph}, the turn sends the graph of
$$x^2 + y^2 = 1$$ onto the graph of $$(xc + ys)^2 + (-xs + yc)^2 = 1$$. Multiply out the left side:

```math
\begin{aligned}
(xc + ys)^2 + (-xs + yc)^2
&= x^2c^2 + 2xycs + y^2s^2 + x^2s^2 - 2xycs + y^2c^2 && \text{multiply out} \\
&= x^2(c^2 + s^2) + y^2(s^2 + c^2) && \text{the } xy \text{ terms cancel} \\
&= x^2 + y^2 && c^2 + s^2 = 1
\end{aligned}
```

The last step is :ref{#trigonometry/pythagorean-identity}. So the new equation is $$x^2 + y^2 = 1$$
again, and the turned circle is the unit circle.

:::

::::

::::exercise{#ex-turning-graphs-half-turn}

Use :ref{#turn-graph} to turn the parabola $$y = x^2$$ by $$\pi$$ about $$O$$, and check the answer
with the point $$(2, 4)$$.

:::solution

Here $$\cos\pi = -1$$ and $$\sin\pi = 0$$ (:ref{#trigonometry/quarter-values}), so the turned-back
point is

```math
(x \cdot (-1) + y \cdot 0,\ -x \cdot 0 + y \cdot (-1)) = (-x, -y)
```

Putting $$-x$$ for $$x$$ and $$-y$$ for $$y$$ in $$y = x^2$$ gives $$-y = (-x)^2 = x^2$$, which is
$$y = -x^2$$: the cup upside down. The half turn sends $$(2, 4)$$ to $$(-2, -4)$$, and
$$-(-2)^2 = -4$$. The answer matches the flip in the $$x$$-axis, because the parabola is its own
mirror image in the $$y$$-axis, and the half turn is those two flips one after the other.

:::

::::

::::exercise{#ex-turning-graphs-circle-centre}

Turn the circle $$(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 1$$ a quarter turn anticlockwise about $$O$$. Find the new
equation and its centre.

:::solution

For $$\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$$ the turned-back point is $$(y, -x)$$, as in the guide. Put $$y$$ for
$$x$$ and $$-x$$ for $$y$$ (:ref{#turn-graph}):

```math
(y - 2)^2 + (-x)^2 = 1 \qquad\text{which is}\qquad x^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 1
```

This is the circle with centre $$(0, 2)$$ and radius $$1$$ (:ref{#coordinates/circle-equation}). The
quarter turn sends the old centre $$(2, 0)$$ to $$(0, 2)$$ (part 4 of
:ref{#trigonometry/radian-turns}), as it should.

:::
