These exercises use the whole chapter, and most need ideas from more than one guide. Try each one
before you open its solution, and name the rule or result behind each step, as the guides do.

Most of them come down to one habit: write the points as letters for as long as you can, rearrange
with the rules for adding and scaling, and put in coordinates only at the end. The letters show why
an answer comes out the way it does, and the coordinates check it.

## Steps and midpoints

::::exercise{#ex-points-solve}

Let $$A = (2, -1)$$ and $$B = (-3, 4)$$.

1. Find $$3A - 2B$$.
2. Find the point $$X$$ with $$2X + A = B$$.
3. Find $$d(A, B)$$.

:::solution

1. By :ref{#scalar-multiple}, $$3A = (6, -3)$$ and $$2B = (-6, 8)$$, so by :ref{#point-difference}
   $$3A - 2B = (6 - (-6), -3 - 8) = (12, -11)$$.
2. Subtract $$A$$ from both sides, which :ref{#difference-step} allows, to get $$2X = B - A$$. Then
   multiply both sides by $$\frac{1}{2}$$, using :ref{#scale-twice}:

   ```math
   X = \tfrac{1}{2}(B - A) = \tfrac{1}{2}(-5, 5) = \left(-\tfrac{5}{2}, \tfrac{5}{2}\right)
   ```

   Check: $$2X + A = (-5, 5) + (2, -1) = (-3, 4) = B$$.

3. By :ref{#distance-norm},
   $$d(A, B) = |B - A| = |(-5, 5)| = \sqrt{25 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$$.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-fourth-corner}

Three corners of a parallelogram, in order round it, are $$A = (1, 1)$$, $$B = (4, 2)$$ and
$$C = (5, 5)$$. Find the fourth corner $$D$$, and check that the two diagonals have the same
midpoint.

:::solution

Going round the parallelogram, the step from $$A$$ to $$D$$ should be the same as the step from
$$B$$ to $$C$$. So $$D = A + (C - B) = (1, 1) + (1, 3) = (2, 4)$$.

The diagonals are $$AC$$ and $$BD$$. By :ref{#midpoint-formula} their midpoints are

```math
\tfrac{1}{2}(A + C) = \tfrac{1}{2}(6, 6) = (3, 3) \qquad \tfrac{1}{2}(B + D) = \tfrac{1}{2}(6, 6) = (3, 3)
```

They agree, as :ref{#parallelogram-law} says they should. In fact $$D = A + C - B$$ was bound to
work for any three corners, since then $$B + D = A + C$$, so the two midpoints are always equal.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-third}

Let $$A = (1, 2)$$ and $$B = (7, -1)$$, and let $$P = A + \frac{1}{3}(B - A)$$.

1. Find $$P$$.
2. Show that $$P$$ lies on the segment $$AB$$, a third of the way from $$A$$ to $$B$$.

:::solution

1. $$B - A = (6, -3)$$, so $$P = (1, 2) + (2, -1) = (3, 1)$$.
2. By :ref{#distance-norm} and :ref{#norm-scale},

   ```math
   d(A, P) = \left|\tfrac{1}{3}(B - A)\right| = \tfrac{1}{3}\,d(A, B)
   ```

   Also $$B - P = (B - A) - \frac{1}{3}(B - A) = \frac{2}{3}(B - A)$$ by :ref{#scale-sum-rules}, so
   $$d(P, B) = \frac{2}{3}\,d(A, B)$$. The two distances add up to $$d(A, B)$$, so $$P$$ lies on the
   segment $$AB$$ by rule 1 of :ref{#distance-and-angles/segment-rules}, a third of the way along.

   With the numbers, $$d(A, P) = \sqrt{5}$$, $$d(P, B) = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$$ and
   $$d(A, B) = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$$.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-centroid}

Let $$A$$, $$B$$ and $$C$$ be the corners of a triangle, let $$M = \frac{1}{2}(A + B)$$ be the
midpoint of $$AB$$, and let

```math
G = \tfrac{1}{3}(A + B + C)
```

1. Show that $$G - M = \frac{1}{3}(C - M)$$ and $$C - G = \frac{2}{3}(C - M)$$.
2. Deduce that $$G$$ lies on the segment $$CM$$, two thirds of the way from $$C$$ to $$M$$.
3. The segment from a corner to the midpoint of the opposite side is called a **median**. Explain
   why all three medians of a triangle pass through $$G$$.
4. Check with $$A = (0, 0)$$, $$B = (6, 0)$$ and $$C = (3, 9)$$.

:::solution

1. Since $$A + B = 2M$$, we have $$G = \frac{1}{3}(2M + C)$$. Rearranging with :ref{#sum-rules} and
   :ref{#scale-sum-rules},

   ```math
   G - M = \tfrac{2}{3}M + \tfrac{1}{3}C - M = \tfrac{1}{3}(C - M) \qquad
   C - G = C - \tfrac{2}{3}M - \tfrac{1}{3}C = \tfrac{2}{3}(C - M)
   ```

2. By :ref{#distance-norm} and :ref{#norm-scale}, $$d(C, G) = \frac{2}{3}\,d(C, M)$$ and
   $$d(G, M) = \frac{1}{3}\,d(C, M)$$. These add up to $$d(C, M)$$, so $$G$$ lies on the segment
   $$CM$$ by rule 1 of :ref{#distance-and-angles/segment-rules}, two thirds of the way from $$C$$.
3. The formula for $$G$$ treats $$A$$, $$B$$ and $$C$$ the same way, since the order of a sum
   doesn't matter (:ref{#sum-rules}). So the same argument with the corners relabelled shows that
   $$G$$ is on the median from $$A$$ and on the median from $$B$$ too. The point $$G$$ is called the
   **centroid** of the triangle, and it is the average of the three corners.
4. $$G = \frac{1}{3}(9, 9) = (3, 3)$$ and $$M = (3, 0)$$. Then $$d(C, G) = 6$$, $$d(G, M) = 3$$ and
   $$d(C, M) = 9$$, so $$G$$ is two thirds of the way down the median from $$C$$.

:::

## Length and direction

::::exercise{#ex-points-unit}

Let $$A = (5, -12)$$.

1. Find $$|A|$$ and the unit point $$U$$ that is a positive multiple of $$A$$.
2. Find the point at distance $$26$$ from $$O$$ in the same direction as $$A$$.

:::solution

1. $$|A| = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$$, so by :ref{#direction}

   ```math
   U = \tfrac{1}{13}A = \left(\tfrac{5}{13}, -\tfrac{12}{13}\right)
   ```

2. The point $$26U$$ has length $$26$$ by :ref{#norm-scale}, and it is a positive multiple of $$A$$.
   It is $$26U = 2A = (10, -24)$$.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-length-range}

Let $$A$$ and $$B$$ be points, in the plane or in space, with $$|A| = 3$$ and $$|B| = 6$$.

1. Show that $$3 \le |A + B| \le 9$$.
2. With $$A = (1, 2, 2)$$ in space, find a point $$B$$ with $$|B| = 6$$ for each end of the range.

:::solution

1. The triangle inequality for lengths holds in the plane (:ref{#norm-triangle}) and in space
   (:ref{#space-norm-rules}). It gives the upper end at once: $$|A + B| \le 3 + 6 = 9$$. For the
   lower end, write $$B$$ as the two steps $$A + B$$ and $$-A$$, one after the other:

   ```math
   6 = |B| = |(A + B) + (-A)| \le |A + B| + |-A| = |A + B| + 3
   ```

   So $$|A + B| \ge 3$$.

2. $$|A| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3$$, so this $$A$$ fits. Taking $$B = 2A = (2, 4, 4)$$ gives
   $$|B| = 6$$ and $$|A + B| = |3A| = 9$$. Taking $$B = -2A = (-2, -4, -4)$$ gives $$|B| = 6$$ and
   $$|A + B| = |-A| = 3$$. These use :ref{#space-norm-rules}. The longest sum comes from two steps
   the same way, and the shortest from two steps in opposite directions.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-square}

Let $$A = (3, 1)$$, let $$B = G_{90}(A)$$ be its quarter turn about $$O$$ (:ref{#quarter-turn}), and
let $$C = A + B$$.

1. Find $$B$$ and $$C$$.
2. Show that the four sides of $$O$$, $$A$$, $$C$$, $$B$$ all have the same length, and that the two
   diagonals have the same length and the same midpoint.

:::solution

1. $$B = G_{90}(3, 1) = (-1, 3)$$ and $$C = (3, 1) + (-1, 3) = (2, 4)$$.
2. The squared sides are

   - $$d(O, A)^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$$
   - $$d(A, C)^2 = 1 + 9 = 10$$
   - $$d(C, B)^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$$
   - $$d(B, O)^2 = 1 + 9 = 10$$

   The diagonals have $$d(O, C)^2 = 4 + 16 = 20$$ and $$d(A, B)^2 = 16 + 4 = 20$$, and both
   midpoints are $$\frac{1}{2}(2, 4) = (1, 2)$$ by :ref{#midpoint-formula}. The angle at $$O$$ is a
   right angle by :ref{#quarter-turn}, so the shape is a square with side $$\sqrt{10}$$. The quarter
   turn builds a square on any step from the origin in the same way.

:::

## Moves as formulas

::::exercise{#ex-points-circle-turn}

The circle $$C$$ has centre $$(4, -1)$$ and radius $$3$$. Find the equation of its image under the
half turn about $$Q = (1, 2)$$.

:::solution

The half turn is $$H_Q(P) = 2Q - P$$ (:ref{#half-turn-formula}), and it keeps every distance
(:ref{#formula-isometries}). A point is on $$C$$ exactly when it is $$3$$ from the centre, so its
image is $$3$$ from the image of the centre, which is

```math
2Q - (4, -1) = (2, 4) - (4, -1) = (-2, 5)
```

Every point at distance $$3$$ from $$(-2, 5)$$ is the image of a point of $$C$$, since $$H_Q$$
undoes itself. So the image is the circle with centre $$(-2, 5)$$ and radius $$3$$, whose equation
is $$(x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 9$$ by :ref{#coordinates/circle-equation}.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-find-zoom}

A zoom $$Z_{r,Q}$$ sends $$(1, 1)$$ to $$(5, -1)$$ and sends $$(0, 4)$$ to $$(2, 8)$$. Find $$r$$
and $$Q$$.

:::solution

The zoom multiplies distances by $$r$$ (:ref{#zoom-formula-distances}). The two points are
$$\sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}$$ apart, and their images are $$\sqrt{9 + 81} = \sqrt{90} = 3\sqrt{10}$$
apart, so $$r = 3$$.

Now $$Z_{3,Q}(1, 1) = Q + 3\big((1, 1) - Q\big) = (3, 3) - 2Q$$. Setting this equal to $$(5, -1)$$
gives $$2Q = (3, 3) - (5, -1) = (-2, 4)$$, so $$Q = (-1, 2)$$. Check the other point:
$$(-1, 2) + 3(1, 2) = (2, 8)$$.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-glide}

Let $$F(x, y) = (x + 1, 2 - y)$$.

1. Write $$F$$ as a flip in an axis followed by a slide, and explain why $$F$$ is an isometry.
2. Show that $$F$$ has no fixed points.
3. Show that $$F \circ F$$ is a slide, and find it.

:::solution

1. $$R_1(x, y) = (x, -y)$$, and sliding that by $$(1, 2)$$ gives $$(x + 1, 2 - y)$$. So
   $$F = T_{(1, 2)} \circ R_1$$. Both keep distances (:ref{#formula-isometries}), so $$F$$ does too
   (:ref{#isometries/composite-isometry}).
2. A fixed point would need $$x + 1 = x$$, which no number satisfies.
3. $$F(F(x, y)) = F(x + 1, 2 - y) = (x + 2, 2 - (2 - y)) = (x + 2, y)$$. So $$F \circ F$$ is the
   slide by $$(2, 0)$$. A move that flips, then slides along its mirror, is a glide reflection
   (:ref{#isometries/glide-reflection}). Here the mirror is the line $$y = 1$$ and the slide is
   $$1$$ to the right, which is why doing it twice slides by $$2$$.

:::

## Combining moves

::::exercise{#ex-points-zoom-then-turn}

Let $$Q = (3, 1)$$, and let $$F = H_Q \circ Z_2$$: zoom by $$2$$ about the origin, then half turn
about $$Q$$.

1. Show that $$F(P) = (6, 2) - 2P$$.
2. Find the one fixed point $$C$$ of $$F$$.
3. Show that $$F(P) = C - 2(P - C)$$. In words, $$F$$ is a zoom by $$2$$ about $$C$$ followed by a
   half turn about $$C$$.

:::solution

1. $$F(P) = H_Q(2P) = 2Q - 2P = (6, 2) - 2P$$, by :ref{#half-turn-formula}.
2. $$C = (6, 2) - 2C$$ gives $$3C = (6, 2)$$, so $$C = \left(2, \frac{2}{3}\right)$$.
3. $$C - 2(P - C) = 3C - 2P = (6, 2) - 2P = F(P)$$. So $$F$$ sends the step $$P - C$$ to
   $$-2(P - C)$$, measured from $$C$$, which is a zoom by $$2$$ and a half turn
   (:ref{#negative-multiple}) about $$C$$. Combining a zoom about one point with a half turn about
   another gave a move of the same kind about a third point.

:::

::::exercise{#ex-points-flip-slide}

1. Show that $$R_1(P + A) = R_1(P) + R_1(A)$$ for all points $$P$$ and $$A$$.
2. Deduce that $$R_1 \circ T_A \circ R_1 = T_{R_1(A)}$$.
3. What slide is $$R_1 \circ T_{(2, 3)} \circ R_1$$? Explain the answer in words.

:::solution

1. With $$P = (x, y)$$ and $$A = (a_1, a_2)$$, both sides are $$(x + a_1, -y - a_2)$$.
2. For any point $$P$$, using part 1 and the fact that $$R_1 \circ R_1$$ moves nothing
   (:ref{#isometries/reflection-twice}),

   ```math
   R_1\big(T_A(R_1(P))\big) = R_1\big(R_1(P) + A\big) = R_1(R_1(P)) + R_1(A) = P + R_1(A)
   ```

   which is $$T_{R_1(A)}(P)$$.

3. It is the slide by $$R_1(2, 3) = (2, -3)$$. Seen in a mirror held along the $$x$$-axis, a slide
   up and to the right looks like a slide down and to the right, as :ref{#conjugate-slide} found for
   zooms and half turns.

:::
