  What zooming does to lengths, areas and volumes, from pizza sizes to the area of a disc.

Imagine a giant ten times your height, built exactly like you. It sounds unstoppable, but its legs
would snap under its own weight. Its bones would be $$100$$ times as strong as yours, while its body
would be $$1000$$ times as heavy. In this chapter we'll see where those numbers come from, using one
idea: what a zoom does to lengths, to areas and to volumes.

The picture to keep in mind is a photocopier set to enlarge. Every length on the page is multiplied
by the same number, so the shape stays the same. The areas grow by that number twice over, and in
space the volumes grow by it three times over. Graph paper is the second picture: you measure an
area by counting the little squares inside a shape, and a zoom turns every little square into a
bigger one.

As in the earlier chapters, a few facts are taken as given and marked clearly in a box, such as "a
shape inside another has no more area". Everything else is proved from them, using the distances,
areas and angles of [Distance and angles](../distance-and-angles/introduction.mdx) and the zooms of
[Isometries](../isometries/introduction.mdx). This chapter also settles two questions those chapters
left open:

- **The area of a disc**: Distance and angles took the formula $$\pi r^2$$ as given. Here it is
  proved, with $$\pi$$ defined as the area of a disc of radius $$1$$, and the same $$\pi$$ turns out
  to give the length of every circle.
- **Zooms and distance**: Isometries left open that a zoom by $$r$$ multiplies every distance by
  $$r$$. Here two special cases are proved from earlier rules, and the general rule is taken as
  given. The [Coordinates](../coordinates/introduction.mdx) chapter proves it.

Some arguments need shapes cut into smaller and smaller pieces. The guides describe in words and
pictures how the error shrinks, and say plainly which steps are taken from the picture. Calculus
makes these steps precise later.

## Guides in this chapter

The chapter has nine guides:

1. [Zooming and length](./scaling-lengths.mdx): a zoom multiplies every length by the same number,
   from map scales and paper sizes to shadows and winding roads
2. [Measuring area with a grid](./grids.mdx): inner and outer counts of squares, and why a finer
   grid pins the area down
3. [Zooming and area](./scaling-areas.mdx): twice as wide means four times the area, for rectangles,
   triangles and every other shape
4. [The area of a disc](./disc-area.mdx): $$\pi$$ as the area of the unit disc, why it lies between
   $$2$$ and $$4$$, and the proof of $$\pi r^2$$
5. [Around the circle](./circumference.mdx): polygons inside a circle, and why the circumference is
   $$2\pi r$$ with the same $$\pi$$
6. [Stretching one way](./stretching.mdx): pulling a shape in one direction, and the area of an
   ellipse
7. [Volume and the square-cube law](./volume.mdx): volume grows by the cube of the zoom, and what
   that means for giants, ice and animals
8. [Test yourself](./test-yourself.mdx): a multiple choice quiz on the ideas in the chapter
9. [Exercises](./exercises.mdx): exercises on the whole chapter, with worked solutions

**Zooming and length**

| Rule                        | Statement                                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#zoom-ray}             | For $$P$$ and $$Q$$ on one ray from the centre, $$d(rP, rQ) = r \cdot d(P, Q)$$               |
| :ref{#zoom-right-angle}     | If $$\angle POQ$$ is a right angle, $$d(rP, rQ) = r \cdot d(P, Q)$$                           |
| :ref{#zoom-rules}           | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies every distance by $$r$$, sends lines to lines and keeps angles     |
| :ref{#zoom-twice}           | Zooming by $$r$$ and then by $$s$$ is zooming by $$rs$$; zooming by $$\frac{1}{r}$$ undoes it |
| :ref{#curve-length-scaling} | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies the length of every curve, and every perimeter, by $$r$$           |

**Measuring area with a grid**

| Rule                | Statement                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#area-compare} | A region inside another has no more area; an isometry keeps area                               |
| :ref{#grid-squeeze} | inner count $$\le$$ area $$\le$$ outer count, for any grid                                     |
| :ref{#edge-band}    | Every square that overlaps $$S$$ but isn't wholly inside lies within $$c\sqrt{2}$$ of the edge |
| :ref{#grid-rule}    | As the squares shrink, the counts get as close as you like, so they pin down the area          |

**Zooming and area**

| Rule                     | Statement                                                                      |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| :ref{#rectangle-scaling} | A zoom by $$r$$ turns an $$a$$ by $$b$$ rectangle into an $$ra$$ by $$rb$$ one |
| :ref{#triangle-scaling}  | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies the area of a triangle by $$r^2$$                   |
| :ref{#area-scaling}      | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies the area of every shape by $$r^2$$                  |

**The area of a disc**

| Rule                     | Statement                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#pi-bounds}         | $$2 < \pi < 4$$                                                               |
| :ref{#disc-area-formula} | A disc of radius $$r$$ has area $$\pi r^2$$                                   |
| :ref{#sector-scaling}    | A zoom about the centre keeps the fraction of the disc that a sector takes up |

**Around the circle**

| Rule                         | Statement                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#inscribed-area}        | A regular polygon in a circle has area $$\frac{1}{2} \cdot$$ perimeter $$\cdot h$$ |
| :ref{#circumference-formula} | A circle of radius $$r$$ has length $$2\pi r$$                                     |
| :ref{#pi-ratio}              | For every circle, circumference divided by diameter is $$\pi$$                     |

**Stretching one way**

| Rule                     | Statement                                                              |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#stretch-rectangle} | A $$w$$ by $$h$$ rectangle lined up with $$L$$ becomes $$w$$ by $$ah$$ |
| :ref{#stretch-area}      | A stretch by $$a$$ multiplies the area of every shape by $$a$$         |
| :ref{#ellipse-area}      | An ellipse with half-widths $$a$$ and $$b$$ has area $$\pi ab$$        |

**Volume and the square-cube law**

| Rule                   | Statement                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#volume-rules}    | A box has volume $$abc$$; pieces add; moving keeps volume; zooms work in space as in the plane |
| :ref{#box-scaling}     | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies the volume of a box by $$r^3$$                                      |
| :ref{#volume-scaling}  | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies the volume of every solid by $$r^3$$                                |
| :ref{#surface-scaling} | A zoom by $$r$$ multiplies surface area by $$r^2$$                                             |
| :ref{#square-cube}     | A zoom by $$r$$ divides the surface for each unit of volume by $$r$$                           |
