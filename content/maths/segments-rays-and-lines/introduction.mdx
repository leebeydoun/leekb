title: Introduction
description:
  Lines written as a starting point plus a number of steps, and the rules for lines proved from
  that.
---

Imagine watching a delivery van on a map in your phone, a dot creeping along a straight road. The
app doesn't store the whole road. It knows where the van started and the step it makes each minute,
and from those two things it can say where the van will be after any number of minutes, whether it
passes your door, and how close it comes if it doesn't. A straight line described this way, as a
starting point plus some number of steps, is called a **parametric** line. In this chapter we'll
describe segments, rays and lines like this, and use the description to prove the facts about lines
that earlier chapters took from the picture.

The idea to keep in mind is the point $$P + tA$$. Here $$P$$ is where you start, $$A$$ is the step,
and $$t$$ is how many steps you take. Letting $$t$$ run from $$0$$ to $$1$$ gives a segment, letting
it run over every number from $$0$$ up gives a ray, and letting it be any number at all gives a
whole line. Questions about lines then turn into equations for $$t$$, and the algebra of the earlier
chapters answers them.

Nothing new is taken as given. Everything is proved from the adding, scaling and lengths of
[Adding and scaling points](../adding-and-scaling-points/introduction.mdx), the distance formula of
[Coordinates](../coordinates/introduction.mdx) and the solving of
[Linear equations](../linear-equations/introduction.mdx) and
[Quadratics](../quadratics/introduction.mdx). Along the way the chapter settles questions that
several earlier chapters left open:

- **Linear equations draw lines**: [Linear equations](../linear-equations/introduction.mdx) took it
  from the picture that $$ax + by = c$$ is a straight line. Here it is proved, both ways round.
- **The rules for lines and segments**:
  [Distance and angles](../distance-and-angles/introduction.mdx) took as given that two points fix
  one line, that each point has one parallel and one perpendicular to a line, and three rules about
  segments. Here every one of them is proved.
- **Zooms and slides**: a zoom about any point keeps each point on its ray from the centre, a slide
  moves every point in the direction of its arrow, and every move of
  [Moves as formulas](../adding-and-scaling-points/moves-as-formulas.mdx) sends lines to lines.
- **Parallelograms**: the opposite sides of the parallelogram built from two steps are parallel.
- **Lines and circles**: any line meets a circle twice, once or not at all, and a tangent meets the
  radius at a right angle.

Two things still come from the picture: measuring angles in degrees, and area. Angles get formulas
with trigonometry, in the [Trigonometry](../trigonometry/introduction.mdx) chapter, which also
proves that a zoom keeps angles.

## Guides in this chapter

The chapter has ten guides:

1. [Segments](./segments.mdx): the points a fraction of the way from $$P$$ to $$Q$$, arrows and
   their steps, and segments moved by slides and zooms
2. [Rays and direction](./rays.mdx): rays as formulas, when two steps point the same way, an arrow
   of a given length, and why zooms and slides move points along rays
3. [Lines](./lines.mdx): lines as $$P + tA$$, the line through two points, parallel lines, where two
   lines meet, and when two moving ships collide
4. [Equations of lines](./line-equations.mdx): turning $$P + tA$$ into $$ax + by = c$$ and back,
   slopes, and parallel lines from their equations
5. [Lines and circles](./lines-and-circles.mdx): where a line meets a circle, tangents, and lines
   through a point inside a circle
6. [Perpendicular lines](./perpendicular.mdx): the dot product, a test for right angles with numbers
   alone, the closest point of a line, and tangents meeting the radius square on
7. [The rules for lines, proved](./rules-proved.mdx): every rule the picture gave about lines,
   segments, rays, parallels and perpendiculars, proved from the formulas
8. [Lines in space](./space.mdx): segments, rays and lines with triples, and lines that miss each
   other without being parallel
9. [Test yourself](./test-yourself.mdx): a multiple choice quiz on the ideas in the chapter
10. [Exercises](./exercises.mdx): exercises on the whole chapter, with worked solutions

**Definitions**

| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| :ref{#segment-formula}       | The points $$P + t(Q - P)$$ with $$0 \le t \le 1$$                                   |

| :ref{#ray-formula}           | The points $$P + tA$$ with $$t \ge 0$$                                               |
| :ref{#same-direction}        | $$B = cA$$ with $$c > 0$$; opposite directions with $$c < 0$$                        |
| :ref{#line-formula}          | The points $$P + tA$$ for every real number $$t$$                                    |
| :ref{#parallel-formula}      | Directions with $$B = cA$$ for some $$c \ne 0$$                                      |
| :ref{#tangent-line}          | A line meeting a circle in exactly one point                                         |
| :ref{#dot-product}           | $$A \cdot B = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2$$                                                      |
| :ref{#perpendicular-formula} | Directions with $$A \cdot B = 0$$                                                    |
| :ref{#space-lines}           | Segments, rays and lines with triples                                                |

| :ref{#skew-lines}            | Lines in space that are not parallel and never meet                                  |
| :ref{#space-dot}             | $$A \cdot B = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$$                                             |
| :ref{#space-perpendicular}   | Lines in space that meet, with $$A \cdot B = 0$$                                     |

**Segments**

| Rule                     | Statement                                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#fraction-along}    | $$X = P + t(Q - P)$$ has $$d(P, X) = t\,d(P, Q)$$ and $$d(X, Q) = (1 - t)\,d(P, Q)$$               |
| :ref{#segment-symmetric} | The segments $$PQ$$ and $$QP$$ are the same, with $$t$$ swapped for $$1 - t$$                      |
| :ref{#arrow-equivalent}  | $$\overrightarrow{PQ}$$ and $$\overrightarrow{MN}$$ are equivalent exactly when $$P + N = Q + M$$  |
| :ref{#segment-slide}     | $$T_A$$ and $$Z_r$$ send the segment $$PQ$$ onto the segment between the images of $$P$$ and $$Q$$ |

**Rays and direction**

| Rule                        | Statement                                                                                                |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#ray-multiple}         | Directions $$A$$ and $$cA$$ with $$c > 0$$ give the same ray                                             |
| :ref{#rays-equal}           | Two rays from $$P$$ are the same exactly when their directions are the same direction                    |
| :ref{#same-direction-rules} | Same direction behaves like equality                                                                     |
| :ref{#direction-unit}       | Same direction exactly when the unit points are equal                                                    |
| :ref{#arrow-given}          | The arrow from $$M$$ of length $$\ell$$ in direction $$A$$ ends at $$M + \frac{\ell}{\lvert A \rvert}A$$ |
| :ref{#zoom-on-ray}          | $$Z_{r,Q}(P)$$ lies on the ray from $$Q$$ through $$P$$                                                  |
| :ref{#slide-direction}      | $$T_A$$ moves every point the distance $$\lvert A \rvert$$ in the direction of $$A$$                     |

**Lines**

| Rule                          | Statement                                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-descriptions}      | Any non-zero multiple of $$A$$ and any point of the line describe the same line |
| :ref{#two-point-line}         | Two different points lie on exactly one line, with direction $$Q - P$$          |
| :ref{#multiples-line}         | The multiples $$tA$$ make up the line through $$O$$ and $$A$$                   |
| :ref{#parallelogram-parallel} | The opposite sides of $$O$$, $$A$$, $$A + B$$, $$B$$ are parallel               |
| :ref{#parallel-determinant}   | Directions are parallel exactly when $$a_1b_2 - a_2b_1 = 0$$                    |
| :ref{#line-intersection}      | Lines that aren't parallel meet once; parallel lines are equal or never meet    |

**Equations of lines**

| Rule                      | Statement                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-equation}      | The line $$P + tA$$ is the equation $$a_2x - a_1y = a_2p_1 - a_1p_2$$              |
| :ref{#equation-line}      | $$ax + by = c$$ is the line through any solution with direction $$(-b, a)$$        |
| :ref{#parallel-equations} | $$ax + by = c$$ and $$a'x + b'y = c'$$ are parallel exactly when $$ab' - a'b = 0$$ |

**Lines and circles**

| Rule                     | Statement                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-circle}       | A quadratic in $$t$$ whose discriminant says the line meets the circle twice, once or never |
| :ref{#inside-point-line} | Every line through a point inside a circle meets it twice, once on each side                |
| :ref{#on-circle-line}    | A line through a point of the circle meets it again, or is tangent there                    |

**Perpendicular lines**

| Rule                         | Statement                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#dot-rules}             | $$A \cdot B = B \cdot A$$, sums and multiples come out, $$A \cdot A = \lvert A \rvert^2$$                         |
| :ref{#difference-length}     | $$\lvert A - B \rvert^2 = \lvert A \rvert^2 + \lvert B \rvert^2 - 2\,A \cdot B$$                                  |
| :ref{#perpendicular-dot}     | The angle at $$O$$ in the triangle $$O$$, $$A$$, $$B$$ is right exactly when $$A \cdot B = 0$$                    |
| :ref{#normal-direction}      | $$(a, b)$$ is perpendicular to $$ax + by = c$$; two such lines are perpendicular when $$aa' + bb' = 0$$           |
| :ref{#perpendicular-slopes}  | Slopes $$m$$ and $$m'$$ give perpendicular lines exactly when $$mm' = -1$$                                        |
| :ref{#closest-point}         | $$F = P + t_0A$$ with $$t_0 = \frac{(X - P) \cdot A}{\lvert A \rvert^2}$$ is the closest point to $$X$$           |
| :ref{#distance-to-line}      | The distance from $$(x_0, y_0)$$ to $$ax + by = c$$ is $$\frac{\lvert ax_0 + by_0 - c \rvert}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$$ |
| :ref{#tangent-perpendicular} | A line through a point of a circle is tangent exactly when it is perpendicular to the radius                      |

**The rules for lines, proved**

| Rule                              | Statement                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| :ref{#line-rule-two-points}       | Two different points lie on exactly one line                                                       |
| :ref{#line-rule-parallel}         | Exactly one line through a point is parallel to a given line                                       |
| :ref{#rules-proved-crossing}      | Parallel means equal or never meeting; crossing lines and parallel to a parallel                   |
| :ref{#product-bound-equality}     | $$as + bt = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\sqrt{s^2 + t^2}$$ exactly when $$(s, t) = c(a, b)$$, $$c \ge 0$$      |
| :ref{#triangle-equality}          | $$d(P, M) = d(P, Q) + d(Q, M)$$ exactly when $$Q$$ is on the segment $$PM$$                        |
| :ref{#rules-proved-parameters}    | One parameter for each point, and segments are intervals of parameters                             |
| :ref{#segment-rules-proved}       | The three rules for segments hold                                                                  |
| :ref{#ray-agrees}                 | The formula ray and the picture's ray are the same                                                 |
| :ref{#perpendicular-rules-proved} | One perpendicular through a point, perpendicular to parallels, and two perpendiculars are parallel |
| :ref{#rules-proved-pythagoras}    | $$A \cdot B = 0$$ exactly when $$\lvert A - B \rvert^2 = \lvert A \rvert^2 + \lvert B \rvert^2$$   |
| :ref{#moves-keep-lines}           | Slides, half turns, zooms, flips and the quarter turn send lines, rays and segments to the same    |
